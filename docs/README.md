# OctoAcme Project Management Docs

This folder contains the core project management guidance used by OctoAcme. The team follows a structured lifecycle that begins with initiation, moves into planning, then execution, release, and continuous improvement. The goal is to keep work aligned to customer value, reduce ambiguity, and give stakeholders a clear view of progress, risks, and outcomes.

OctoAcme starts by validating the business need through a one-pager that captures the problem, stakeholders, goals, success metrics, risks, and rough timeline. Once approved, work is planned into a backlog with priorities, delivery milestones, dependencies, and a definition of done. During execution, the team uses regular standups, weekly syncs, PR review expectations, and QA/testing gates to move work forward while managing blockers early. Release activities focus on staging validation, deployment checks, rollback readiness, and stakeholder communication. After each sprint or milestone, the team conducts a retrospective to capture learning and turn those actions into ongoing improvement.

## Core roles and responsibilities

OctoAcme emphasizes clear ownership across product, project, and delivery functions. Product managers define customer value and prioritize the backlog, while project managers coordinate schedules, risk management, documentation, and stakeholder communication. Developers build and test features to meet acceptance criteria, and QA/testing validates quality and acceptance before release. This separation of responsibilities helps teams move quickly while keeping decision-making transparent and accountable.

## Communication and quality practices

Communication is built around regular cadence and escalation paths. The team uses daily standups to surface progress and blockers, weekly project updates to review risks and milestones, and stakeholder communications for status and decisions. Risk and dependency management are tracked in a formal register and reviewed regularly so issues can be addressed before they create major delivery disruption.

Quality assurance is treated as a continuous part of delivery. OctoAcme expects unit and integration tests, smoke tests for critical flows, CI security checks, and manual QA when business acceptance needs human validation. Release readiness requires passing CI, clear acceptance criteria, documented rollback plans, and smoke testing before production deployment. This combination of governance, communication, and quality controls helps the team deliver reliably and improve over time.
