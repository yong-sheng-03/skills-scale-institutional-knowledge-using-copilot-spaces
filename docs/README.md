# OctoAcme Project Management Docs

This folder contains the project management guidance for OctoAcme. The team follows a structured lifecycle that starts with initiation, moves into planning, then execution, release, and retrospective improvement. The goal is to keep work aligned to customer value, reduce ambiguity, and make progress visible to everyone involved.

OctoAcme begins by validating the business need through a project one-pager that documents the problem, key stakeholders, success metrics, rough timeline, and initial risks. Once the initiative is approved, planning turns those ideas into a prioritized backlog, milestones, dependencies, and a definition of done. During execution, work is tracked through daily standups, weekly reviews, PR expectations, and quality checks that surface blockers early and keep delivery moving. When a feature is ready, the team shifts to release-readiness activities such as staging validation, deployment checks, rollback planning, and stakeholder communication.

## Roles and shared accountability

Clear roles help the team maintain ownership throughout the lifecycle. Product managers define the problem, prioritize the backlog, and measure outcomes. Project managers coordinate schedules, dependencies, communications, and risk management. Developers implement and test features to meet acceptance criteria, while QA/testing validates quality and acceptance before release. This combination of role clarity and shared accountability keeps the team aligned while enabling fast decision-making across functions.

## Communication and quality practices

OctoAcme emphasizes regular communication and escalation paths. Daily standups keep the team coordinated on progress and blockers, while weekly project syncs review risks, milestones, and interdependencies. Stakeholder communication is maintained through a single source of truth, and escalations move from the team to PM, product lead, and sponsor when issues become business-critical. Risk management is documented in a risk register and reviewed throughout the project so mitigation actions can happen early.

Quality is treated as a continuous part of delivery rather than a final gate. The process expects unit tests, integration tests where appropriate, smoke tests for critical user flows, security scanning in CI, and manual QA when acceptance requires human validation. Release readiness requires passing CI, release notes, rollback documentation, and post-deploy verification. After each sprint or milestone, the team holds a retrospective to capture what went well, what should improve, and which action items will be tracked in the backlog.
